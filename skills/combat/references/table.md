# Table procedure and checkpoint

## Player-facing citations

Use short citations such as “SRD 2.0, p. 53” during play. If a clickable source is
useful, link the official PDF with its page fragment:
`https://www.daggerheart.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/DH_SRD_2_2026_08_25.pdf#page=53`.
Internal reference links locate packaged data for you; do not expose installation,
cache or filesystem paths as player-facing source links.

## Dice

At the start, agree on who supplies rolls. Portable default: the player rolls
physical dice or a dice application and reports results for both PCs and GM.
Request Hope and Fear d12 values separately, plus other dice and modifiers. For
enemy attacks request a d20; for damage name the exact dice expression.

If an actual random-roll tool exists and the player agrees, use it and report
its result. Never describe model-composed numbers as random dice. A predeclared
test value or player-selected number must be labeled as such. A diceless or
deterministic variant is a separate table choice, not a hidden fallback.

Settle intent, stakes and pre-roll spending first. Ask for missing mechanical
inputs rather than silently choosing a favorable value. If a rule permits a
post-roll change, allow that choice before narrating irreversible consequences.

## Compact sheet

Record identity, level/tier, class/subclass stage, ancestry/community, traits,
Experiences and modifiers; Evasion; HP/Stress capacities and marks; available Hope
and capacity; GM Fear; Armor Score and marked slots; Major/Severe thresholds;
equipped weapon traits/hands/range/damage with Proficiency; source feature/card
names, acquired/active/vaulted cards, Recall costs; limited uses, conditions and
their clearing triggers; class/companion resources; selected advancements and
remaining slots when verified; inventory and unresolved provisional choices.

## Visible checkpoint

On request (including “checkpoint” or “save”), at a session ending or after enough
complex play, write a compact, copyable Markdown checkpoint in the conversation:

1. **Campaign:** title, SRD edition, tone, agreed optional/house rules and dice policy.
2. **Current scene:** location/time, immediate situation and whose decision is pending.
3. **Characters:** exact current sheets/resource changes and selected options.
4. **Encounter:** individual enemy state, ranges, conditions, effects and GM Fear.
5. **Continuity:** established events, discoveries known to players, relationships,
   inventory changes and consequences. Mark assumptions/proposals separately.
6. **Pressures:** visible countdown values, triggers and stakes; limited-use refresh rules.
7. **Pending:** unanswered choices and provisional rulings that still need validation.

Do not expose undiscovered secrets, prepared twists or unrevealed NPC motives.
Keep visible clues and established facts sufficient to reconstruct play; secrets
not preserved by the player may be lost with the conversation. Explain that a
checkpoint is a text record, not a backend save. Continue from it without resetting
resources, leveling, completing rests or adopting suggested events automatically.
